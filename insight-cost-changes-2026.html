Client briefing / July 2026

Upcoming changes to construction costs

A number of new levies, taxes and regulatory changes will affect the cost of building in England over the next two years. This briefing sets out the most significant, in date order. Some of the earlier dates have now passed; we have left them in because they still govern schemes that applied before them.

B

Timeline

In date order

DateChangeEffect on cost
30 Sep 2026Second staircaseTwo staircases become mandatory for new residential buildings over 18 m. Extra core cost and lost saleable or lettable area, with a real effect on tall-building viability.
1 Oct 2026Building Safety LevyNew levy on schemes of 10 or more dwellings, or 30 or more PBSA bedspaces, in England, paid to the local authority before completion. Charged per m² of chargeable GIA at district rates, from £6 to £100/m² nationally; Birmingham is £14.62 brownfield and £29.23 greenfield. Affordable housing and schemes under ten units are exempt.
1 Jan 2027UK CBAMCarbon tax on imported aluminium, cement, fertiliser, hydrogen, iron and steel, charged on embodied emissions at a rate tracking the UK carbon price. Expect supplier increases on carbon-intensive materials; seek fixed-price quotations on steel and cement packages where possible.
24 Mar 2027Future Homes and Buildings StandardNew homes with no gas boilers (heat pumps assumed), solar PV and upgraded fabric. The Government estimates an average uplift of about £4,350 per dwelling, with a parallel specification uplift for non-domestic buildings. Applies to higher-risk (18 m+) buildings from 24 Sep 2027.
1 Apr 2027Scottish Building Safety LevyScotland introduces its own levy.
1 Jan 2028Waste disposal joins UK ETSEnergy-from-waste incineration joins the UK Emissions Trading Scheme. Gate fees for residual and mixed waste are forecast to rise by about £48/t (roughly 50%), and skip and muck-away rates for non-inert waste will follow.
By 2031MEES for commercial landlordsThe EPC C by 2027 milestone has gone. The new target is EPC B by 2031 for let commercial buildings over 1,000 m²; smaller lets stay at a minimum of EPC E.

Worked example

100 units, Birmingham brownfield

25 affordable and 75 market units, with the building control application submitted after 24 March 2027.

Building Safety Levy, after deducting exempt affordable floorspacec.£93,000 to £104,000
Future Homes Standard uplift, 100 dwellings at £4,350c.£435,000
Total additional costc.£530,000 to £540,000
Per market dwellingc.£7,000 to £7,200

The levy range reflects the assumption on chargeable communal GIA. Confirm the gross to net with the design team; the levy is charged on chargeable GIA under the RICS Code of Measuring Practice, 6th edition.

Timing

Three dates that could be beaten

  • Building Safety Levy: a building control application submitted before 1 October 2026 escaped the levy on that application entirely.
  • Future Homes Standard: submit building control documents before 24 March 2027 and start the works before 24 March 2028 to avoid the c.£4,350 per dwelling uplift.
  • Second staircase: 18 m+ schemes with a building control application in before 30 September 2026, and foundations started by 30 March 2028, keep a single-stair design.
D

Already in current prices

April 2026 statutory rises

Landfill Tax is £130.75/t standard and £8.65/t lower rate; Aggregates Levy is £2.16/t; Building Safety Regulator time on 18 m+ projects is £156/hr; planning fees rose 3.8%. CITB Levy rates are unchanged at 0.35% of PAYE and 1.25% of net CIS.

Also on the horizon

The proposed Infrastructure Levy has been dropped, so CIL and Section 106 both remain. A Nature Restoration Levy, the successor to nutrient neutrality, is expected to roll out catchment by catchment from late 2026, with no rates published yet. Biodiversity Net Gain exemption changes took effect on 6 August 2026: the self-build exemption for nine units or fewer was removed and a new 0.2 ha exemption introduced. Planning application fees now rise with CPI every April, and MHCLG is consulting on letting councils set higher fees of their own.

On 18 m+ residential schemes, budget contingency for Building Safety Regulator approval times and fees at each gateway. First-time Gateway 2 approval rates have been reported at around 10%.

Compiled from GOV.UK and legislation.gov.uk sources, verified 19 July 2026. General guidance only, not legal or project-specific advice. Confirm rates and dates for a specific scheme before relying on them.
