Payment and pay less notices, in plain terms

Most commercial arguments on a construction project start with a notice that was late, missing or unclear. The rules are short, and a party who follows them every month avoids most of that argument.

B

The timetable

The Housing Grants, Construction and Regeneration Act 1996, as amended by the Local Democracy, Economic Development and Construction Act 2009, gives every construction contract a payment mechanism built around two dates. The due date is when a payment becomes due; the final date for payment is the last day on which it can be paid.

No later than five days after the due date, the payer gives a payment notice stating the sum it considers due and the basis on which that sum is calculated. If the payer wants to pay less than the notified sum, it gives a pay less notice before the final date. Under the standard JCT contracts that is no later than five days before the final date; where the Scheme for Construction Contracts applies, it is seven days.

When a notice is missed

If no payment notice is given, the payee's notice becomes the notified sum. Under the JCT contracts, an interim application made on or before the due date counts as that notice. If no pay less notice follows either, the notified sum must be paid by the final date, whatever the payer thinks the work is worth. The argument about true value can still be had, but only after the money has been paid.

A payee who is not paid the notified sum by the final date can suspend performance after giving at least seven days' notice of its intention, and is entitled to its reasonable costs and an extension of time for the period of suspension.

How we run it

We set the payment timetable out at the start of the job, with every valuation date, due date and notice date for the contract period in one schedule. Each month the valuation is issued as a combined payment notice and, where needed, pay less notice, presented net to net off the previous notice so the figures read straight across.

When we take over a scheme part way through, the first thing we check is the notice history, because a missed notice months earlier can still decide what is owed today.

General guidance only, not legal advice. Always check the payment terms of the contract in question.
