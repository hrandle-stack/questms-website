Reinstatement cost assessments: a note for managing agents

The buildings sum insured on a block is usually set by the managing agent, and it is the figure most likely to be out of date. A reinstatement cost assessment puts a defensible number behind it.

B

What an RCA is

A reinstatement cost assessment is an estimate of what it would cost to rebuild the building if it were destroyed. It has nothing to do with market value. A flat in a city centre block can sell for less than its share of the rebuild cost, and a period building in a conservation area can cost far more to rebuild than it would fetch.

Most buildings in the UK are insured on a day one basis. The assessment gives the cost of reinstatement at the start of the policy period, which becomes the declared value; the insurer then adds an allowance for cost inflation over the policy year and over the time it would take to design, approve, tender and rebuild.

What goes into the figure

A proper assessment covers more than the building itself: demolition and site clearance, temporary works and making safe, professional and statutory fees, compliance with the Building Regulations that would apply to the rebuild (not the ones that applied when the block was built), external works within the insured boundary, and VAT where it cannot be recovered. On a higher-risk building, the time and cost of the Building Safety Regulator’s gateway approvals belong in the figure as well. A figure taken from a square metre rate alone tends to miss most of this.

Why it matters

Most buildings policies carry an average clause. If the building is insured for less than its true reinstatement cost, the insurer pays only the same proportion of any claim, partial losses included. A block insured for 70% of its rebuild cost recovers 70% of the cost of a roof fire. That shortfall falls on the freeholder and, through the service charge, on the leaseholders.

Rebuild costs have not stood still. BCIS reported that residential rebuild costs rose by 4.9% in the year to January 2026, after 3.8% the year before, so a sum insured that has only been index-linked since a pre-2022 assessment can be well adrift.

How often

The usual recommendation is a full reassessment every three years, with an annual desktop review against cost indices in between, and a fresh assessment sooner after any significant alteration, extension or change of use.

What we need from you

  • The address and the extent of the insured property, including car parks, commercial units and outbuildings.
  • Any drawings or lease plans you hold, and the current insurance schedule with the existing sum insured.
  • Access for an inspection, with a contact on site for plant rooms and roof areas.
  • Details of any works carried out since the last assessment.
  • Whether you need the figure apportioned between blocks, units or service charge schedules.

We carry out the inspection, measure the building where drawings are missing or unreliable, and issue a report with the declared value, the basis of assessment and the assumptions behind it, in a form your broker and insurer can use. Fees are fixed in advance, with reduced rates for agents instructing across a portfolio.
