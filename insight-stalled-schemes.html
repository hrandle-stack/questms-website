Taking over a stalled scheme: the first month

When a contractor or construction manager leaves a part-built project, the decisions taken in the first few weeks decide how much the rest of it costs. These are the checks we run, roughly in the order we run them.

B

Who holds which role

Read the contract and every notice served on it. Establish whether the termination was valid, who is still named as contract administrator, employer’s agent or construction manager, and whether anyone has been formally replaced. An instruction or certificate issued by someone the contract does not recognise invites an argument later, so a replacement is put in place by notice before anything else is signed.

Safety and insurance on day one

Someone has to take on the principal contractor’s duties under CDM 2015 the day the contractor leaves, and someone has to insure the works. Both are easy to overlook in the rush, and on a stalled site the works insurance has often lapsed with the contract. We confirm both in writing before any trade returns to site.

The money

We rebuild the payment history from the notices actually issued and the sums actually paid, which are rarely the same thing. That shows what has been certified, what has been paid twice, and where a missed notice still governs what is owed. It is the starting point for the final account with the outgoing party and for every trade valuation that follows.

Materials and title

Paid-for materials, on or off site, need to be identified, marked and secured, with evidence of title. Where a supplier or trade is holding goods back, the contract and the payment record decide whether they can.

The supply chain

Each package is dealt with on its merits. Trades well advanced and performing are usually brought across by novation or a direct contract; trades that have finished are left in place with a warranty obtained for their work; packages with no signed contract are re-let. The aim is that every trade on site has a contract with the right party and a clear payment route.

Cost to complete

Finally, an honest cost to complete: committed and uncommitted costs, prelims run-off for the realistic programme and a contingency that reflects what is still unknown. Funders and their monitoring surveyors need a figure they can rely on month after month, and an early realistic number is worth more than an optimistic one that has to be revised.

General guidance only, not legal advice. Every stalled project turns on its own contract and records.
